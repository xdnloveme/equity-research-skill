# Eight Financial-Statement Capabilities

For each capability retain findings, evidence, reasoning/calculations, forecast implications, and limitations. Put material findings and explanatory bridges in the relevant report section; keep detailed reconciliations and the F01–F08 coverage record in linked Workpaper A. Adapt metrics to the business; an inapplicable ratio does not make the entire capability inapplicable.

## F01 Definitions and Statement Reconciliation

**Question:** Are observations economically comparable, and do accounts, model, and market definitions reconcile?

**Inputs:** Statements, equity movements and notes, accounting framework, fiscal calendar, restatements/disposals/acquisitions, shares/EPS, segment eliminations, FX, and non-GAAP reconciliations.

**Analysis:**
1. Build a dictionary of period, stock/flow, cumulative/standalone quarter, units, currency, reported/adjusted definition, and actual/estimate status. Prefer comparable restatements only if published by the cutoff; preserve their impact.
2. Derive standalone quarters from comparable cumulative figures within the same year. Do not mechanically add quarterly diluted EPS into annual EPS. Handle 52/53-week years and different fiscal calendars.
3. Reconcile assets to liabilities plus equity; reconcile opening cash, cash movements, FX and relevant restricted-cash definitions to closing cash. Reconcile income attribution, preferred claims where relevant, and weighted-average shares to EPS.
4. Reconcile segment revenue/profit to group totals, including eliminations and corporate costs; bridge reported and adjusted earnings.
5. Use appropriate share counts/dates for market cap, EV, and EPS. Rebuild from the latest capital structure, including new preferred/convertible claims, issuance and actual repurchases; do not carry forward an old buyback or dilution narrative. Explain FX translation rather than silently applying one closing rate to all flows.

**Outputs:** Definition table, reconciliations and differences, comparable history, and unresolved items restricting forecasts/ratings.

**Boundary:** Distinguish rounding from material inconsistency. Never force a balance with unexplained “other.” Reconciliation is not an audit or comprehensive factual verification.

## F02 Revenue Growth Attribution

**Question:** Is growth driven by demand, price, mix, acquisitions, FX, or recognition timing, and is it repeatable?

**Inputs:** Revenue and segments, volumes/customers/usage/prices, bookings/delivery, acquisition/FX contributions, recognition policy, contract balances, and channel evidence.

**Analysis:**
1. Choose a valid driver equation: volume×price, average customers×ARPU, or transaction volume×take rate. Period-end customer counts do not equal period averages.
2. Separate organic/inorganic/disposal effects, constant-currency/reporting growth, calendar effects, and major recognition changes.
3. Build a bridge. For two factors, ΔR=ΔQ×P0+Q0×ΔP+ΔQ×ΔP; a disclosed sequential method is also valid. Do not double-count interactions; show mix at the business level and retain unexplained residuals.
4. Compare absolute growth contributions as well as share changes; distinguish existing-customer expansion, new customers, pricing, penetration, and new markets.
5. Corroborate with usage, orders, inventory, budgets, and delivery. Backlog requires analysis of cancellation, timing, and enforceability before conversion to revenue.

**Outputs:** Growth bridge, primary drivers and persistence, leading indicators, and explicit M08 assumptions.

**Boundary:** Use disclosed proxies or ranges for unavailable price/volume data; do not present inferred ASP or customer metrics as reported facts.

## F03 Margins and Operating Leverage

**Question:** Why did earnings grow faster or slower than revenue, and what is structural versus cyclical or temporary?

**Inputs:** Revenue/COGS, gross profit, expenses, segment profit, prices/mix, materials/labor, utilization/yield, depreciation, R&D, selling costs, SBC, FX, and other income.

**Analysis:**
1. Locate changes from revenue through gross profit, operating profit, pre-tax income, attributable income, and EPS; keep margin definitions distinct.
2. A consistent gross-profit bridge is ΔGP=ΔR×GM0+R1×ΔGM. Attribute margin changes to price, mix, costs, efficiency, depreciation, and FX, disclosing the decomposition order.
3. Distinguish fixed, variable, and staged spending. A lower expense ratio does not imply lower absolute expense or permanently improved efficiency.
4. Classify structural, cyclical, one-off, timing, and accounting effects, avoiding overlapping attribution.
5. Forecast from a normalized observed starting point, explaining material demand/mix, pricing, efficiency and cost changes and their timing. Use a cost build or a reasoned top-down margin estimate according to available disclosure; explain its direction and scale. Reconcile calculations and identify material residuals, but do not invent granular costs to fill them or use a backsolved residual as independent validation. Test the main profit drivers; exact cost attribution is not a prerequisite for a forecast.

**Outputs:** Profit bridge, margin attribution including residuals, sustainable margin range, and forecast revisions.

**Boundary:** Rising sales with falling margin does not prove “growth costs”; EPS surprise alone does not identify an operating-margin improvement.

## F04 Earnings Quality and Accounting Judgments

**Question:** Do earnings reflect recurring economics, or are adjustments and accounting estimates overstating them?

**Inputs:** Recognition/contract notes, capitalized development and other costs, useful lives, bad-debt/inventory/warranty provisions, restructuring/impairment, investment/fair-value gains, tax items, and non-GAAP reconciliations.

**Analysis:**
1. Identify material policies/estimates affecting recognition timing or value and quantify current/future effects where possible.
2. Bridge reported income to the sustainable measure used in research; identify material adjustments, related tax effects, cash status and recurrence. Match normalized earnings and tax treatment; label tax estimates where an exact allocation is unavailable.
3. Recurring restructuring or SBC is not costless because management excludes it. Explain treatment of acquired-intangible amortization and other adjustments consistently across history, forecast, and valuation.
4. Compare receivables, contract assets, inventories, and capitalized costs with activity; investigate alternative explanations rather than labeling anomalies as fraud.
5. Sensitize material accounting estimates and distinguish economic changes from recognition changes.

**Outputs:** Reported-to-adjusted bridge, rationale, recurrence assessment, accounting risks, and EPS/FCF/value effects.

**Boundary:** Adjusted earnings are not presumed superior. Reconcile accounting-framework and non-GAAP differences before comparisons.

## F05 Cash Flow and Working Capital

**Question:** Does profit become cash, and is cash performance operating, investment-related, or timing-driven?

**Inputs:** CFO, noncash adjustments, receivables/inventory/payables, contract assets/liabilities, factoring/supply-chain finance, cash taxes/interest, capex, leases, and restricted cash.

**Analysis:**
1. Bridge net income through noncash and working-capital adjustments to CFO using clear cash-release/use signs.
2. Compare seasons and periods, separating recurring conversion from settlements of prior accruals, exceptional cash items and working-capital timing. Show reported and diagnostic cash flows without reversing actual cash balances or assuming legal/restructuring payments never recur. Where useful, DSO=average receivables/credit sales×days; DIO=average inventory/COGS×days; DPO=average payables/purchases×days.
3. Disclose proxies such as total sales for credit sales or COGS for purchases. Acquisitions, FX, and write-downs can make balance-sheet changes differ from cash-flow movements.
4. Assess whether cash improvement comes from inventory liquidation, stretched payments, prepayments, or financing arrangements and whether it can persist.
5. Define FCF explicitly. CFO less cash capex is a useful starting convention, not a universal valuation cash flow. Check lease/interest classifications; disclose noncash asset additions and future payments. Build consistent FCFF/FCFE for DCF where used.

**Outputs:** Net income→CFO→defined FCF bridge, working-capital contributions, cash-conversion trends, and forecast assumptions.

**Boundary:** Prepayments carry fulfillment obligations; do not deduct the same lease payment twice. Adding SBC back in CFO does not eliminate its shareholder cost; align dilution/value treatment.

## F06 Investment, Capital Returns, and Growth Quality

**Question:** What investment is required, when does it pay off, and can returns exceed the cost of capital?

**Inputs:** Cash/noncash capital investment, construction/PPE, depreciation, leases, R&D/customer acquisition, acquisitions, subsidies, commercialization/capacity schedules, utilization, and invested capital.

**Analysis:**
1. Separate material investment paths by their economics, including fixed assets, development, customer acquisition, acquisitions or working capital as relevant. Identify who funds the initial outlay and whether continued growth requires further cash advances. Keep research reclassifications separate from reported accounts.
2. Trace investment→delivery/use→revenue recognition→profit and collection with realistic lags, distinguishing evidence of demand, profitability and cash recovery. Contracts or recognized revenue alone do not establish collection; missing precise payback dates do not invalidate a reasoned path. For physical assets distinguish cash payment, acquisition, commissioning and depreciation. Reconcile additions and PPE using capital creditors/prepayments, noncash leases, acquisitions, disposals and FX where material; cash capex is not automatically additions, and unexplained residuals remain visible.
3. Split maintenance/growth spending only where supportable; otherwise use disclosed uncertainty or ranges.
4. Where operating invested capital is economically meaningful, analyze ROIC=NOPAT/average invested capital, defining operating tax, excess cash, goodwill, and leases; compare with a compatible capital-cost assumption.
5. Do not equate short-term ΔNOPAT/Δcapital to a mature project IRR. Account for acquisitions, the cycle, and delayed returns.
6. Stress demand, price, utilization, delay, and costs. For asset-light businesses assess R&D/acquisition efficiency; any analytical capitalization must not overwrite statutory statements.

**Outputs:** Investment mix, evidenced payoff stage and timing, material return sensitivity, relevant capital-return measures, and conditions under which growth creates value.

**Boundary:** ROE can rise from leverage, repurchases, or shrinking equity; that alone does not establish operating improvement. Where funding and required capital are integral to operations, use a compatible capital-return measure rather than mechanically applying industrial ROIC.

## F07 Balance Sheet and Funding Resilience

**Question:** Can the company meet obligations and essential investment under stress without forced financing or dilution?

**Inputs:** Available/restricted cash, debt maturities/rates/currencies, leases, covenants, guarantees, committed spending, contingent liabilities, credit-facility conditions, distributions, and stressed cash generation.

**Analysis:**
1. Build a time-based funding sources/uses schedule, separating essential spending and contractual obligations from adjustable investment and distributions. Separate freely available from constrained funds; inspect restrictions, marketability and valuation risk in cash and current investment securities. Current classification alone does not establish funding availability.
2. Assess suitable net-debt, coverage, and covenant headroom metrics. Separate operating losses from liquidity gaps; uncommitted financing is not assured funding.
3. Stress demand, collections, interest/FX, and refinancing costs consistently with M13's downside case.
4. Explain which uses defined FCF can cover, how any residual gap is funded, and the cost/dilution or distribution implications, including buybacks and SBC. Use a break-even funding calculation only when it clarifies the decision, not as a new forecast or a costless spending-cut assumption.

**Outputs:** Funding timeline, maturity pressures, liquidity headroom, potential financing/dilution paths, and rating implications.

**Boundary:** Positive FCF need not be distributable cash after other uses; a funding need alone does not prove value destruction, nor available funding attractive returns. Missing covenant information limits a “no financing risk” claim.

## F08 Earnings Surprise and Independent Forecasting

**Question:** What exceeded prior expectations, can it persist, and how does our forecast differ from the market?

**Inputs:** Comparable actuals, prior guidance, fixed pre-release consensus snapshots, available post-release revisions, F01–F07, business drivers, and shares.

**Analysis:**
1. Compare separately with historical periods, prior guidance, and pre-release consensus. Missing consensus prevents beat/miss claims, not other analysis.
2. Attribute EPS differences to revenue, operating margin, financing/other income, tax, attribution, and shares. Use absolute differences when percentage comparisons near zero or with negative bases would mislead.
3. Separate sustainable operating upside, timing, noncore gains, and buyback effects before extrapolation.
4. Model drivers→segment revenue/profit→corporate/financing/tax→attributable profit→diluted EPS, with consistent working capital, investment, cash, debt, and shares. Three EPS guesses alone are not a model.
5. Compare external forecasts after checking dates, sample coverage, periods, revenue definitions and F04 earnings adjustments; publication after results does not prove every contributor updated. Explain material revenue, cost, tax, adjustment and share differences where information allows; do not reconstruct an unavailable broker model as fact. Challenge a material opposing thesis as a coherent investment/delivery/revenue/cost/funding path: a cost-only shock does not test a growth-and-investment case. Retain or revise our path using evidence, not the initial snapshot or consensus median. Label inferred inputs as implied, never disclosed. V02 owns earnings/multiple matching.
6. Translate forecast differences into EPS/FCF/value and assess what price already reflects. Do not double-count earnings revisions and rolling to a later fiscal year.

**Outputs:** Prior-expectations comparison, revision bridge, independent forecasts, consensus differences where available, sensitivities, confidence, and gaps.

**Boundary:** A forecast may legitimately match consensus, but that is not differentiated insight. Highly unverified drivers limit conclusion strength; precision does not resolve uncertainty.
