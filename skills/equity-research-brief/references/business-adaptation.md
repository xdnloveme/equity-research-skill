# Operating Stage and Business Adaptation

Keep the research questions and all eight financial capabilities; select drivers and valuation methods to match economic reality. Select inputs from its disclosed economics, not an issuer or industry preset.

## Operating stage and research emphasis

**Inputs:** Operating history, product adoption and paying demand, unit economics, revenue/profit/cash trends, reinvestment, funding runway and material segment differences. Market capitalization, revenue scale and liquidity describe size; they do not establish operating stage.

**Analysis:** Classify the economic stage from observed behavior, identify the strongest contrary evidence, and state what would change the classification. Do not impose unsupported size or growth-rate cutoffs. A small company can be mature; a large company can still be scaling. Classify material businesses separately when a group contains different stages, reconciling them with reported segments rather than inventing segment data.

| Operating stage | Evidence to assess | Research emphasis | Implications for method selection under V02 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seed / commercial validation | Paying demand or product-market fit unproven; short history; losses, uncertain unit economics or dependence on new funding | Adoption and commercialization milestones, unit economics, cash runway, survival, financing and dilution; do not turn TAM into revenue without a delivery path | Milestone/conditional scenarios and suitable comparable or risk-adjusted cash-flow methods where supported. Negative earnings invalidate PE, not all valuation. DCF requires especially careful survival and maturation assumptions; early stage does not make it more reliable |
| Growth / scaling | Demand partly established; rapid expansion; margins, reinvestment and competitive position still changing | Retention/orders and delivery, organic growth, operating leverage, capital productivity, financing and the path to sustainable cash generation | Earnings/EV multiples, segment valuation or multi-stage DCF depending on forecast comparability and evidence for the transition. Revenue multiples need credible eventual margins and funded growth |
| Mature / cash generation | Repeatable business economics and cash generation; established competitive position and capital-allocation history | Earnings durability, competitive erosion, cash conversion, replacement investment, distributions and returns from new initiatives | Earnings multiples, cash-flow valuation, distributions or segment methods can be primary or supporting. Stable cash flows may support DCF well; size is not a reason to exclude it |

Treat cyclicality, distress/turnaround and regulatory capital as overlays. Normalize cycle profits rather than calling peak earnings mature cash generation; analyze restructuring/funding or regulatory capital rather than forcing a steady-growth endpoint. A mature group entering a new investment cycle needs separate assessment of the new activity and legacy cash flows. "Seed" describes the economics of a listed issuer/business here; it does not expand the skill into private financing execution.

**Outputs:** In M03, a short company/business stage assessment, supporting and contrary evidence, reclassification triggers, and the few questions receiving deeper analysis with their module locations. Research emphasis is qualitative by default, not 17 arbitrary percentage weights. Adapt unavailable history and inapplicable ratios with reasons while retaining all eight financial capabilities. M13 owns method roles and any valuation weights; avoid repeating its method table here.

## Economic drivers and capital needs

**Inputs:** Disclosed business/segment activity, contracts, customer behavior, cost structure, asset responsibility, working capital, commitments and cash movements. Reconcile economic businesses to reported segments before adding detail.

**Analysis:** For each material business, answer the following using observable inputs or clearly bounded proxies. Keep only the drivers needed to explain its economics; no predefined industry checklist is required.

| Question | Analysis / required link |
|---|---|
| What creates paying demand and recognized revenue? | Identify the measurable activity base, price/monetization, retention or repeat demand, mix, delivery and recognition timing; connect leading indicators to revenue with supported lags |
| What allows the company to retain profit? | Link customer buying criteria, alternatives and bargaining power to pricing, costs, retention and duration; distinguish fixed, variable and staged costs |
| What resources must be funded, and by whom? | Map owned, leased, outsourced, shared or customer-provided resources; include physical and intangible investment, working capital and commitments. Outsourcing or low reported capex does not prove zero capital needs |
| When does investment become output and cash? | Link payment, acquisition/development, availability, use, expense/depreciation and collections. Separate existing resource headroom from new investment and replacement needs where supported |
| What constrains cash available to shareholders? | Assess essential reinvestment, restricted funds, capital requirements, debt/other claims and financing/dilution; select accounting and return metrics that fit these constraints |

Allocate shared costs/assets only with disclosed use or supported ranges; reconcile consolidated totals and eliminate overlap. Revenue or profit shares alone do not prove capital weights. Undisclosed segment assets or spending remain estimates, not facts. A group capital return is an outcome to reconcile, not an input imposed on every business.

**Outputs:** One driver/capital map, with definitions, timing, evidence limits and links to M08 forecasts, M10 funding/investment and M13 method selection. Reuse the map in those modules rather than maintaining another model here. Financial mechanics remain in F01–F08; V02 owns parameter calibration.

## Boundaries

Unavailable operating drivers or segment profit require a disclosed proxy, range, or limitation; do not substitute “industry standard” guesses as facts. TAM requires a bridge through serviceable demand, share, price, capacity, delivery, and funding. Growth/share gains must separately demonstrate profit and cash returns. M09 roadmap, M10 investment, M11 concentration, and M12 competition retain their economic questions regardless of the business model.
