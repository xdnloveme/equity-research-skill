# Research Module Specifications

This file defines questions, inputs, analysis, and outputs. Financial mechanics reside in financial-analysis.md; pricing and rating rules reside in valuation-rating.md. Preserve IDs when translating the deliverable. SKILL.md owns materiality, report/workpaper placement and acceptance; outputs below describe the complete research, not mandatory tables in the narrative.

## M00 Research Scope, Definitions, and Evidence

**Question:** What security and information set are being analyzed, and are the numbers comparable?

**Inputs:** Security identity, quotes and timestamps, filings/notes, restatements, guidance, consensus snapshots, and event publication dates.

**Analysis:** (1) Freeze cutoff, timezone, investment horizon, share class, and quote status. (2) Assign source IDs and record reporting period, publication/access date, units/currency, definitions, and evidence class. (3) Execute F01; resolve conflicts explicitly and list missing inputs. Exclude and record post-cutoff materials in historical work, including later restatements.

**Outputs:** Header conventions and direct citations with decision-relevant limits in the report; the source/definition index, coverage ledger, material-event ledger and two-pass freshness log in linked workpapers. An institution's name alone is not a usable citation.

**Source-use discipline:** Use this framework to structure the investigation; do not import issuer facts, parameters or conclusions from example reports as defaults. For material financial facts, retrieve the underlying filing/release and reproduce relevant calculations where accessible. A secondary source can locate evidence or supply otherwise unavailable data, but label its origin, definition and remaining verification gap. Treat consensus as a measurement of expectations, third-party industry estimates as estimates, and broker multiples/targets/ratings as external judgments to challenge—not facts establishing fair value. Several articles repeating one report are one evidentiary origin, not independent corroboration. Search for contrary evidence and resolve conflicts by provenance, comparability and explanatory support, not source count or agreement with the current thesis. Official disclosure is not automatically unbiased: distinguish reported results, management guidance and promotional claims; cross-check material claims with customer, competitor, regulator or other independent observations where available. Missing primary access limits the affected claim rather than making all secondary evidence unusable.

## M01 Investment Conclusion and Rating

**Question:** At the stated price and horizon, why buy, hold, avoid, or withhold a rating?

**Inputs:** M05 theses, M08 forecast, M13 valuation/rating, M14 catalysts, M15/M16 risks and falsification.

**Analysis:** (1) Separate business quality, earnings outlook, and price attractiveness. (2) Use M13 results without recalculating under different assumptions; distinguish preliminary and final ratings. (3) Connect the conclusion to price, horizon, evidence, and downside; identify earnings, valuation, and distribution contributions. Do not invent personalized position sizes without portfolio context.

**Outputs:** Decision card: quote/date, target horizon, bear lower bound–base price/band–bull upper bound, price changes and total returns, one main rating and its decisive evidence under V05, separately labeled material sensitivity interval, price thresholds, theses and review triggers. Use V03/V05 meanings and conditional-only treatment under NR.

## M02 Company and Security Snapshot

**Question:** What is the company's current economic and financial scale, and how is it priced?

**Inputs:** Price, period-end shares/share classes, ADR terms, FX, market cap, cash/debt, latest annual/quarterly/LTM accounts, guidance, and forecasts.

**Analysis:** (1) Separate market-cap share counts from weighted-average EPS shares and address multiple classes. (2) Summarize revenue, earnings, margins, cash generation, capital returns, and suitable valuation metrics; label meaningless negative-denominator multiples. (3) Separate guidance, analyst estimates, and available consensus with A/E, period, currency, and unit labels.

**Outputs:** Compact snapshot table and timing definitions. For cross-listed securities, show actual quotes separately; a price ratio containing an ADR premium is not a spot FX quote.

## M03 Business Model and Value Creation

**Question:** Who pays, for what benefit, through which mechanism, and why can this company retain profit?

**Inputs:** Products/services, customers/channels, monetization, contracts, costs, assets, resources, value-chain position, operating history, adoption, unit economics and funding dependence.

**Analysis:** (1) Map each major business to customer need, delivery, pricing, cost, and profit source. (2) Choose an economically valid revenue equation and identify demand, supply, distribution, funding, or regulatory constraints. (3) Examine unit economics and scale effects; reconcile economic businesses with reported segments and shared costs. (4) Apply business-adaptation.md to assess operating stage, material business differences and research priorities; keep company size separate from stage.

**Outputs:** Business-model table, revenue/profit driver tree, stage assessment with evidence/counterevidence and reclassification triggers, and two to four decisive operating questions with primary analysis locations. A product inventory alone is insufficient. Put method roles and weights in M13.

## M04 Revenue Mix and Growth Sources

**Question:** Which activities generate current revenue and incremental growth, and what does the mix shift change?

**Inputs:** Segment/product/service/application/customer-type/geographic revenue, amounts and shares, volume/price/customer metrics, and comparable history.

**Analysis:** (1) Use at least two meaningful available dimensions; label estimates for undisclosed dimensions. (2) Execute F02; distinguish absolute growth, mix movement, and growth contribution. (3) Connect mix to margin, cyclicality, capital intensity, and value; never add overlapping classifications of the same revenue.

**Outputs:** Multi-period mix table, growth bridge, and forward sensitivities aligned with M08.

## M05 Investment Theses and Expectations Gap

**Question:** What are the decisive investment debates, and where does our judgment differ from expectations?

**Inputs:** Customer demand and willingness to pay, company value capture, product/adoption and competitor evidence from M09/M12, financial diagnosis, dated expectations, valuation, and sensitivities.

**Analysis:** (1) Select two to four decision-critical debates, not a list of products or risks. For each, explain customer benefit/demand → why this company captures the opportunity → volume, price, mix or cost → incremental earnings and cash → what expectations already discount. Industry growth alone does not establish value capture. (2) Use M09's commercialization milestones and M12's advantage evidence to support the causal links; quantify the material driver and financial contribution or a bounded sensitivity in M08, with explicit residual uncertainty. Separate durable change from cyclical, currency, timing and accounting effects using evidence that could distinguish them. (3) Assess both the value-creation mechanism and its strongest failure mechanism; weight them by evidence, not equal paragraph counts or a presumed bullish/bearish conclusion. Explain whether growth investment is an economic opportunity, an obligation or an unproven bet, referencing M10's payoff/funding analysis. (4) State our view versus identifiable expectations and why the disagreement matters at the observed price. Without reliable consensus, use conditional price-implied assumptions. Assign a verification window and reference M14/M16 for detailed monitoring and falsification.

**Outputs:** Thesis cards or short causal arguments containing claim, customer benefit, company capture mechanism, supporting/contrary evidence and strength, expectations gap, modeled financial/value contribution, timing and linked falsification. Identify widely held views rather than presenting them as proprietary insight. M05 owns the synthesis; do not duplicate M09/M12 evidence tables or M08 calculations here.

## M06 Latest Results and Earnings Trend

**Question:** What changed operationally, and is the earnings change healthy and repeatable?

**Inputs:** Multi-period statements/notes, segment results, operating KPIs, prior guidance, and pre-release consensus.

**Analysis:** (1) Execute F01–F05 and F08's surprise analysis, incorporating F06/F07 where relevant. (2) Compare year-on-year, sequentially with seasonality, against guidance, and against pre-release expectations. (3) Separate structural, cyclical, one-off, timing, and accounting effects and identify forecast changes. A difference between revenue and EPS surprise is not a complete attribution.

**Outputs:** Results comparison, profit bridge, earnings-quality diagnosis, and cash corroboration. Locate capital/financing/forecast findings in M08/M10; the F01–F08 coverage record and detailed reconciliations belong in the workpapers.

## M07 Management Guidance and Near-Term Outlook

**Question:** What does the next-quarter/full-year outlook imply, and how have expectations changed?

**Inputs:** New/prior guidance and assumptions, seasonality, orders/delivery, consensus snapshots and publication dates.

**Analysis:** (1) Calculate midpoint and implied growth using the correct base; distinguish formal guidance, aspiration, and qualitative commentary. (2) Explain concurrent revenue, margin, and investment movements under consistent assumptions. (3) Separate company changes from consensus revisions, constrain observation windows to the cutoff, and consider execution history where relevant.

**Outputs:** Old/new guidance and expectations table, implied assumptions, analyst assessment, and forecast revision. If formal guidance is absent, say so and analyze available leading indicators without relabeling them as guidance.

## M08 Independent Forecast and Expectations Bridge

**Question:** What can the company earn and generate in cash, and how does our model differ from the market?

**Inputs:** F01–F08, operating drivers, roadmap, investment, management guidance, and accessible consensus.

**Analysis:** (1) Build revenue from observed activity and order/recognition constraints, resolving scope, timing and definition changes. Use F03 to bridge profit, shared costs, financing, tax, attribution and dilution. (2) Explain decision-material assumptions as **comparable observed starting point → evidenced or estimated changes and timing → representative base estimate**. Use the same evidence standard for benefits and burdens, explaining their direction, approximate magnitude and duration through demand, competition, delivery, efficiency and costs. Choose the most representative operating path, not the safest defensible outcome. Top-down estimates remain legitimate without granular guidance; neither missing guidance nor temporary headwinds alone justify a lasting forecast discount. (3) Link investment, working capital, depreciation, cash, debt and shares to that path. Apply V02's judgment standard and F08 for subsequent external comparison.

**Outputs:** Normally three historical and two to three detailed forecast fiscal years, segment/quarter detail as needed, driver assumptions, analyst/consensus comparison, EPS and FCF difference bridges, and uncertainties. Extend the operating transition for M13 as needed without duplicating near-term tables. Forecasts must not appear as facts.

## M09 Product, Technology, and Commercialization Roadmap

**Question:** What enables the next growth phase, and what separates announcement from economic delivery?

**Inputs:** Product/service or process roadmaps, customer problem and economic benefit, trials/certification, paid adoption or orders, rollout/capacity, monetization, channels, delivery constraints, and dated company/customer evidence.

**Analysis:** (1) Select material initiatives and distinguish development, customer validation, commercial availability, paid adoption/delivery and scaled profit/cash generation using appropriate evidence for each stage. Publication of a roadmap is not delivery; delivery is not profitable adoption. (2) Explain the customer benefit in cost, performance, reliability, convenience or other relevant terms; separate demonstrated results from company targets and connect the benefit to adoption, willingness to pay or cost savings. (3) Map each decision-critical milestone to a dated window, dependencies and an observable test, then to adoption/volume × realized monetization or another valid driver equation. Account for rollout capacity, yield/utilization, cannibalization, required investment and recognition/collection lags. (4) Identify the contribution already included in M08's base forecast versus conditional upside; do not add product potential on top of a forecast already containing it. Test material delay, weaker adoption or unit-economics failure against the same model. If contribution cannot be bounded, label it unquantified and avoid using it as a quantified target-price uplift.

**Outputs:** Commercialization roadmap with stage/source, customer benefit, next measurable milestone/window, dependencies, forecast driver/contribution and delay or failure effect. Reference M08 for numbers, M10 for capital/payback, and M14 for ongoing monitoring. For non-technology businesses use service, process, channel or store development; retain the same economic questions without forcing a technology lifecycle.

## M10 Capital Allocation, Geographic Footprint, Cash Flow, and Resilience

**Question:** Does investment create value, and can the company fund its obligations and shareholder distributions?

**Inputs:** Capex, development/capitalization, acquisition, leases, location/subsidies, CFO/FCF, debt, dividends, repurchases, and share-based compensation.

**Analysis:** (1) Execute F05–F07; separate maintenance/growth investment only where supportable. (2) Map actual growth investment—capacity, R&D, acquisition, or customer acquisition—to demand evidence, incremental economics and payoff timing; distinguish the company's assets/obligations from those borne by customers or suppliers. Assess both capacity/productivity benefits and underutilization, cost or obsolescence risks; near-term cash consumption proves neither value creation nor destruction. Analyze overseas/local cost differences when material. (3) Stress funding, maturities, commitments, and distributions; distinguish gross repurchases from effects net of dilution. (4) Include material post-filing financing and acquisition developments. Separate confirmed terms from reported discussions; analyze current funding/dilution sensitivities rather than postponing them until the next filing.

**Outputs:** Allocation/payback table, earnings-to-cash bridge, capital-return trend, funding stress table, and per-share effects of distributions and repurchases.

## M11 Customer Concentration, Geography, and Supply Dependencies

**Question:** Which customers, channels, platforms, regions, or suppliers concentrate bargaining power and downside?

**Inputs:** Major-customer shares, channel/platform dependence, customer mix, billed geography/customer headquarters/end demand, production/sourcing, and currencies.

**Analysis:** (1) Separate headquarters, revenue geography, end-market demand, and production location. (2) Analyze concentration trends, alternatives, bargaining power, and correlated demand factors; many customers can share the same demand cycle. (3) Translate delays, disruption, channel change, FX, and regional restrictions into model impacts.

**Outputs:** Concentration/geography table, dependency mechanisms, and stress effects. Consumer businesses may lack a large-customer issue but still have channel/supplier concentration. Disclose missing data.

## M12 Competition and Competitive Advantage

**Question:** What supports superior economics, for how long, and how can it be eroded?

**Inputs:** Customer buying criteria; comparable price/total cost, performance/reliability, delivery scale, share, retention/migration and unit economics; distribution, R&D, ecosystem and regulation; dated issuer, customer and competitor evidence.

**Analysis:** (1) Identify realistic customer alternatives, including incumbents, substitutes, insourcing and doing without the product. Select comparison dimensions that change customer choice; normalize date, product/service scope, workload/cohort, geography and commercial stage before claiming superiority. (2) Distinguish announced capability, customer qualification, commercial delivery and repeatable profitable scale. Use quantified comparisons where available; otherwise state a supported proxy or qualitative limit, not fabricated cost/share precision. Test both advantages and dimensions on which rivals are stronger. (3) Trace an asserted moat to observable pricing, retention, migration friction, unit cost, share or capital returns. Explain customer switching costs and supplier value capture rather than inferring a moat from size, branding, patents or a performance benchmark alone. (4) Assess durability and realistic erosion: partial substitution, second sourcing or negotiating pressure can hurt pricing before wholesale displacement. Link the strongest erosion path and its timing to M08 drivers and M13's premium/discount or excess-return duration, without charging the same effect twice.

**Outputs:** Comparable competitive matrix with source/date and limitations; supported advantage → customer behavior → economic outcome chain; strongest rival advantage, durability/erosion path and forecast implication. Reference detailed M16 triggers. A list of competitors or unmeasured labels such as "ecosystem" is insufficient; missing decisive evidence limits the strength of a moat or valuation-premium claim.

## M13 Valuation, Price, and Dynamic Rating

**Question:** What does the price imply, what base value and main judgment does the evidence support, and how do adverse/favorable outcomes change the decision?

**Inputs:** Verified quote, M08 model, capital structure, valuation comparables/history, dividends, scenarios, and FX.

**Analysis:** (1) Execute valuation-rating.md using M03's business/stage assessment and a single target date and evidence register. (2) Select primary/supporting tools and any method weights under V02; explain evidence quality and method disagreements. Apply DCF-specific requirements only when used. (3) Calibrate a representative base and main rating under V02/V05; test it with coherent bear/bull paths, separately labeled sensitivities and price-implied expectations. Calculate returns and price thresholds without substituting ranges for judgment. Reconcile updates without conflating price, evidence, assumptions and method effects.

**Outputs:** Method-role/evidence table and any weight rationale; forecast metric × multiple or other selected value bridge; dated market comparison; derivation of M01's bear/base/bull prices and returns; parameter evidence and representative-base rationale, separately labeled sensitivities, price-implied and rating-changing conditions, one main rating or NR, entry thresholds, validity and any old-to-new comparison. Include operating-to-equity and DCF transition/terminal bridges only where applicable. Do not impose unused-tool tables on the report.

## M14 Catalysts and Monitoring KPIs

**Question:** What could change expectations, and what signals reveal whether the thesis is working?

**Inputs:** Results dates, operating metrics, roadmap/customer/industry events, estimate revisions, regulation, and capital-allocation decisions.

**Analysis:** (1) Distinguish expectation-changing events from routine monitoring; a scheduled earnings release alone does not establish direction. (2) Define timing/frequency, source, positive/negative signal, affected model variable, and reassessment action. (3) Support thresholds with seasonality, historical variability, guidance, or sensitivities; use disclosed proxies for unobservable metrics.

**Outputs:** Horizon-relevant catalyst/KPI table with threshold rationale. A monitoring plan is not an automation already created.

## M15 Principal Risks and Loss Scenarios

**Question:** What can cause losses, through which mechanisms, and what lies outside ordinary valuation scenarios?

**Inputs:** Demand/concentration, competition, execution, financial/liquidity, FX, legal/regulatory, geopolitical, and continuity exposures.

**Analysis:** (1) Check each risk category and focus on actual company exposure. (2) Translate risks to revenue, margin, reinvestment, financing, or valuation; account for correlated causes. (3) Separate an ordinary operating bear case from suspension, asset destruction, business bans, or other tail events.

**Outputs:** Risk transmission and sensitivity table, loss ranges where supportable, and unquantifiable exposures. The bear target is not a maximum-loss guarantee; risks must affect analysis rather than appear only as boilerplate.

## M16 Contrary Case and Falsification

**Question:** What is the strongest reasonable opposing case, and what evidence would change our mind?

**Inputs:** M05 theses, opposing evidence, competitive/funding constraints, operating thresholds, and sensitivities.

**Analysis:** (1) Build a credible counterargument for each core thesis. (2) Separate warning, review trigger, and falsification; specify measurement window, threshold basis, sources, and seasonal/definition controls. (3) State which model variable, value, or rating changes when triggered. A price decline alone does not falsify a business thesis.

**Outputs:** Thesis–counterargument–trigger–verification–model/rating action table, including early signals insufficient on their own to establish structural change.

## M17 Final Conclusion and Investment Logic Map

**Question:** Do business, financial, and price judgments form one coherent decision?

**Inputs:** Final evidence/model, M01 card, and unresolved items.

**Analysis:** (1) Restate rating, applicable price/horizon, strongest positive/negative reasons without introducing new facts. (2) Map demand→value capture→revenue→profit/cash→per-share value→price/rating and risk entry points. (3) Reconcile headline, text, tables, and conclusion, separating verification from inference.

**Outputs:** Short standalone decision including the same bear lower bound–bull upper bound, base price (or justified band), return and main rating as M01; rendered logic map or readable causal chain and review conditions. Keep sensitivity intervals distinct and conditional values labeled; do not repeat tables. Never deliver drawing source code as the finished diagram.
